# Incorporation — the short version

*UK private company limited by shares, one director, nothing else.*

Everything below is a decision to make, not research to do. The only thing that
needs checking on the day is the price, because the provider's fees move.

---

## The decisions

| | | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | Private limited company (Ltd), limited by shares | A PLC is for companies that raise from the public and have a share structure an insurer understands. This is none of those things. |
| **Name** | Something nobody has taken | Check on the Companies House search before you commit to anything. "Swarm Inspection" will be gone; pick something with a distinctive word in it and be ready to lose it. |
| **SIC code** | **62011** — development of computer software | The company sells an instrument that is, at its core, software: a survey instrument, a coverage statement and a report. If the business becomes a hosted service rather than a product, that is **63122** (data processing and hosting), and you can change it later without a new company. |
| **Director** | One. You. | One is the legal minimum and it is the right number. A second director only matters for a later funding round or for a "key person" argument. |
| **Shares** | 1 ordinary share, £1, 100% you | There is no reason to do anything else. Share counts and classes are paperwork you create for yourself and then have to explain to a due-diligence reader. |
| **Registered office** | **See below — this is the one that matters.** | |
| **Person with significant control** | You, at 100% | Filed at incorporation if you register with a PSC statement on the same day. Do it then; amending later is a separate filing and a separate fee. |
| **Accounting reference date** | 31 December | Standard, and it aligns with the tax year. |
| **Company type** | "Private company limited by shares" | |

## The registered office, and why it is the decision that matters

**Whatever address you put here appears on the public register at Companies
House, and anyone can look it up in about fifteen seconds.**

So the real question is not "what is my registered office" but **"do I want my
home address on the public register of every company in the country"**.

Three options:

1. **Your home address.** Free. Free forever. And permanently public, for the
   life of the company — the register does not care that the company is dormant,
   pre-revenue or two people in a room.
2. **A registered office service.** Somewhere else, and the service takes the
   post and forwards it. Cost: a small annual fee. The catch that matters: the
   service usually requires you to be able to receive *official* post at the
   address it gives you, including anything Companies House sends by hand —
   and it can charge you more if the company does not use it.
3. **An address you already have a reason to be at** — a co-working space with
   a mail service, a friend's office, a yard, a marina, a chandlery. Free, and
   it is at least an address on this industry rather than a PO box.

For a company whose whole commercial argument is *we know what a survey report
should say and you don't*, having the registered office listed as the address of
a Royal Yacht Marina berth or a ship's chandlery is not a joke. It is a
statement, and it costs nothing.

**Pick option 3 if you can, option 1 if you cannot, option 2 if you need
separation.** Whatever you pick, the answer is on a public register forever, so
pick it deliberately and write the reason down.

## The mechanics

### What it costs

Companies House charges a fee for the incorporation itself — £50 online, and
more for same-day service — and the formation provider charges its own fee on
top. Providers that bundle the two (Tide is the obvious one) quote a single
price. **The figure quoted to us was around £15 all-in, but verify it: provider
pricing moves, and the same-day option costs more than the standard one.**

Do not pay for anything you do not need yet:

| do not buy yet | why |
|---|---|
| Same-day incorporation | You are not waiting on a deal. |
| A registered office service | See above — decide on option 3 first. |
| An accountant | Until there is money moving. |
| A business bank account | A Ltd can be formed without one, and a payment-processor account or a second personal account covers the first months. |
| Insurance | There is nothing to insure yet. Professional indemnity only becomes a question when someone pays for a report. |

### The order of operations

1. **Check the name.** Companies House search, including the "names previously
   used" tab. Pick three and take the first that clears.
2. **Decide the registered office** — the decision above, not an afterthought.
3. **Incorporate.** Same-day is fine; standard is fine. Complete the PSC
   statement in the same session, which is free while you are there.
4. **Keep the certificate of incorporation, the articles, and the statement of
   capital.** They arrive as PDFs. Put them in a folder with the date.
5. **Note the three recurring dates:**
   - **Confirmation statement** — every 12 months, online, about £34. Set a
     calendar reminder the day you incorporate. This is the filing companies
     House sends reminder letters about, and it is a fine that grows.
   - **Accounts** — from the first anniversary. Dormant accounts are cheap and
     simple if nothing has happened; a first set of accounts that says
     "nothing" is a legitimate set of accounts.
   - **Corporation tax** — only if a notice arrives. A dormant company is not
     sent CT600s by accident.
6. **VAT** — registration is only required above £90,000 of taxable turnover in
   twelve months. A pre-revenue company does not register, and does not charge
   VAT.

### The traps

- **A company with no transactions still files accounts.** Dormant, cheap, but
  required. Calendar the first one.
- **A confirmation statement is not the annual return** people mean when they
  say it. It is a different, cheaper form, and it is the one that carries the
  fine for being late.
- **The PSC register is separate from the officers list.** Being the sole
  director and the sole owner are two entries in two registers.
- **Sole director = sole signatory.** Every contract, bank instruction and
  filing is signed by one person. That is acceptable, and it is the point at
  which an accountant becomes worth having.

## What the incorporation is actually for

Not to raise money, and not to contract with a customer yet. It is for three
things, and they are worth doing in this order:

1. **It is the cheapest possible way to make the thing credible.** A company
   number on a proposal beats a promise. It tells a chandlery owner or a
   surveyor that this is not a hobby.
2. **It makes the IP belong to the company.** Anything built before
   incorporation belongs to you personally; anything built after belongs to the
   company. Since the product is software and the domain document is the asset,
   incorporate before the next round of building rather than after.
3. **It is the vehicle a grant application or an accelerator needs.** Several
   of the competitions researched for this project require a registered entity
   and, in one case, a sole-director company of a given SIC code. Doing this now
   means the application is a form to fill in rather than a company to form.

**It does not commit you to anything.** A dormant company with one director and
100 ordinary shares costs about £15 and an hour. It can stay dormant for years.
The decision it does commit you to is the registered office — and that one is
worth twenty minutes of thought.

---

**Verify before you pay:** the provider's current price and what it includes;
Companies House's current filing fees; and whether the address you have in mind
can lawfully be used as a registered office.
